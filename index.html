Meet ContosoMart

Before the tools, the story. Every lab is a chapter in one company's push to trust its data — so each exercise has a clear reason to exist.

A coffee retailer that outgrew its plumbing

ContosoMart is a fast-growing online coffee retailer — beans, gear and subscriptions sold across the web, a mobile app and wholesale partners, in four currencies (USD, CAD, EUR, GBP), shipped from three warehouses.

Growth outran the plumbing. The storefront writes orders as JSON; the warehouse exports inventory as CSV; marketing keeps customers in a CRM; finance lives in spreadsheets; operations runs a live SQL database and a cold-store fridge that streams IoT; and a new roastery partner drops a feed into its own cloud storage. Nobody owns the whole picture, and the reports disagree.

The Monday numbers war

Every Monday, leadership argues about last week's revenue — Finance, Operations and Marketing each bring a different number from a different silo. Then a best-seller (the Ethiopia Yirgacheffe) sells out mid-promo because nobody saw the reorder point cross.

"One set of numbers everyone trusts — and I want to see problems before they cost us, not after." — the CEO's mandate

The whole story, end to end

Every lab plugs into one ContosoMart-Analytics workspace and one OneLake. Sources land in the medallion lakehouse; the warehouse, Eventhouse and ML model read the same open Delta copy; Power BI and live dashboards serve it; domains govern it. Then Part 2 puts Azure AI Foundry agents on top, and Part 3 secures them with Microsoft Defender. Press play and follow the thirteen acts — or step through them yourself.

The ContosoMart story

A fictional scenario threads the labs together — each one is the next thing the business needs. Follow the story end to end, or jump straight to any lab.

Act 1 · The mess

Tame the data → Medallion lakehouse

“Nobody agrees on yesterday's sales.” ContosoMart's numbers live in three silos — web orders (JSON), warehouse inventory (CSV) and a CRM export — with mixed dates, currencies and duplicate customers. You build a bronze → silver → gold lakehouse so there's one trusted source, and serve a Direct Lake report.

Act 2 · Peak season

React live → Real-Time Intelligence

“We can't wait for tomorrow's report.” A promo hits: carts are being abandoned right now and a cold-store fridge is warming up. You stream the storefront clickstream and IoT into an Eventhouse, watch a live dashboard, and fire Activator alerts.

Act 3 · No more manual uploads

Automate it → Data Factory ingestion

“Someone re-uploads files every morning.” The medallion works, but ingestion is by hand. You clean a supplier price list with Dataflow Gen2, land daily sales with an incremental pipeline, and orchestrate the whole flow on a schedule.

Act 4 · Finance wants in

Give them T-SQL → Data Warehouse

“Give us SQL, not Spark.” The finance team needs a governed star schema they can query in T-SQL. You stand up a Warehouse with COPY INTO, model the star, and join it back to the lakehouse gold in one cross-database query.

Act 5 · Partners & the ops DB

Don't copy it → Mirroring & Shortcuts

“Don't build another pipeline for that.” A new roastery partner's feed and the live inventory database need analyzing without brittle ETL. You bring them into OneLake zero-copy — a shortcut and database mirroring — then query it all with one engine.

Act 6 · Look ahead

Predict it → Data Science & ML

“How much will we sell next week?” Reporting the past isn't enough. You train a demand forecast on the curated data, track it with MLflow, batch-score with PREDICT, and write the predictions back to gold for BI.

Act 7 · Govern the sprawl

Organize it → Governance & Data Mesh

“Who owns all this — and can we trust it?” The estate now spans six workloads. You organize it into business domains and data products, discover and endorse them in the OneLake Catalog, apply sensitivity labels, and trace lineage end to end.

Do them in order for the full narrative, or jump to any lab — each stands alone. All labs share the ContosoMart-Analytics workspace and lh_contosomart lakehouse, and every module explains the why with links to learn more. You'll need a free Fabric trial for the hands-on steps.

Part 2

Azure AI Foundry — make it agentic

The estate is trusted (Part 1) — now let anyone just ask, and let the systems act. These five acts put Azure AI Foundry agents on top of the same OneLake, using the Fabric data agent as the bridge. New prerequisites: an Azure subscription, and a paid F2+ (or P1+) Fabric capacity — the free trial can't run these. All grounded in the Azure AI Foundry docs.

Act 8 · Ask in plain English

Ask the data → Fabric data agent

“Only people who write SQL can query it.” Turn the governed OneLake estate into a plain-English Q&A experience with a Fabric data agent over gold, the warehouse, the semantic model and the KQL eventhouse — governed, read-only, and ready to publish.

Act 9 · Your first Foundry agent

Build an agent → Foundry Agent Service

“We want an assistant that reasons and acts.” Create an Azure AI Foundry project, deploy a model, and build + test a prompt agent in the portal — the managed runtime you'll ground in ContosoMart's data next.

Act 10 · Give it the numbers

Ground it → Connect Fabric to Foundry

“The agent is smart but makes up numbers.” Attach the Fabric data agent as a tool with identity passthrough, so the Foundry agent answers grounded in the same trusted OneLake data Priya reports on.

Act 11 · Ground it in knowledge

Add knowledge → Foundry IQ

“What's our returns policy?” Add a Foundry IQ knowledge base over ContosoMart documents — policies, contracts, runbooks — for citation-backed answers alongside the Fabric numbers.

Act 12 · Agents that talk to each other

Orchestrate → Multi-agent concierge

“One agent is doing too much.” Split the concierge into specialists — a Fabric numbers agent and a Foundry IQ knowledge agent — behind a router, using Foundry workflows and A2A. The CEO's “just ask, and act” assistant, delivered.

Part 3

Microsoft Defender — secure & govern the agents

The agents are live (Part 2) — now make them safe. This finale puts Microsoft Defender for AI agents over the estate: discover every agent, assess its security posture, detect threats in near-real-time, hunt with Advanced Hunting, and govern its identity and data. New prerequisites: Microsoft Defender XDR with Microsoft Agent 365 onboarding — mostly a guided tour, plus a hands-on Advanced Hunting module. Grounded in the Defender security-for-AI docs.

Act 13 · Secure the agents

Secure it → Defender for AI agents

“The agents act on their own — who's watching them?” Discover every ContosoMart agent, score its posture, detect prompt-injection and secret-leak threats, hunt the AgentsInfo table with KQL, and govern identity (Entra Agent ID) and data (Purview). The whole saga closes: trusted, agentic and secure.
